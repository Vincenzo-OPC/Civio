# CSC competency gap map and evidence

Research date: 21 October 2026. Repository: `dbdf2bc04fe8d7edde4c8b7cf1e87be01138abe1`. Official scope was checked against the CSC announcement PDF, including its two-column scope table. Latest detailed scope retrieved is for August 2026. The 2027 calendar is available; the detailed announcement for the founder's chosen sitting must be rechecked before final mock configuration.

## Official scope versus internal design

| Scope | Professional | Subprofessional |
|---|---|---|
| English and Filipino verbal: meaning, completion, errors, sentence structure, paragraph organization, comprehension | Yes | Yes |
| Numerical: basic operations, sequences, word problems | Yes | Yes |
| General Information: Constitution, R.A. 6713, peace/human rights, environment | Yes | Yes |
| Analytical: analogy, symbolic/abstract reasoning, assumptions/conclusions, data interpretation | Yes | No separate analytical subtest |
| Clerical: filing and spelling, in English | No separate clerical subtest | Yes |

Source S1 publishes a passing **general rating of at least 80.00**. It does not establish that a Civio raw percentage equals a CSC rating, nor publish the domain quotas used by Civio. Preserve separate level blueprints. No exact item probabilities or future question forecasts are supported by these sources. Numerical fractions, ratios, algebra and geometry are educational subskills under the broad scope, not separately enumerated official test weights. Probability and detailed LGU trivia require a scope decision before occupying scarce release slots.

Retain the proposed 300 English / 300 Filipino / 400 Numerical / 400 Analytical / 300 Clerical / 300 GI allocation as an **internal proposal**. It is defensible as a two-level content-library budget, but cannot ensure balanced mastery. Prioritize coverage depth within it, particularly Filipino sentence structure and reading, numerical prerequisites, varied data interpretation and argument reasoning. Do not silently change the target or mirror the Professional allocation in a Subprofessional mock.

## Confirmed depth problems

- Numerical: current age-algebra, new-person averages, perimeter cost, two-share ratios and paired-worker rates repeat narrow tasks. Coverage labels conceal missing foundations and reverse/transfer forms.
- Analytical: 41 table questions all test row totals or cell differences; add appropriate proportions, trends and limits of inference after existing drafts are reviewed. All 39 items tagged ANA-CONCLUSION in the generated pool are ordering tasks, so the label alone does not establish argument-conclusion coverage. Symbolic series has 77 items, versus 30 analogies across six tags.
- English: 53 current reading-tagged items versus 113 meaning/completion-word items; passage quality and inference depth still require independent editorial review. Do not count different questions sharing one passage as independent reading texts.
- Filipino: 188 approved-labeled items, including 35 reading-tagged and 16 paragraph-tagged items. Multiple naming systems (VFIL-PAGBASA / VFIL-READ etc.) obstruct reliable coverage counting. Shared grammatical tags need separation into error recognition and sentence structure.
- Clerical: filing conventions must be explicit, particularly multiword surnames. A duplicate-choice historical spelling item demonstrates why stable IDs and orthographic review matter.
- GI: 60 deadline/number questions versus 21 environment-principle questions and other mixed tags. Tag counts alone do not prove peace/human-rights application depth or current law accuracy.

## Operational competency blueprint

The [44-node blueprint](data/competency-blueprint.json) records exam level, subject, skill, subskill, micro-skill, prerequisites, archetypes, common misconceptions, objectives, cognitive demand and mastery assessment. It comprises 22 numerical nodes, 12 bilingual verbal nodes, four analytical, two clerical and four GI nodes. The micro-skills are educational recommendations within the published scope, not additional official CSC subject lists.

Every official verbal area appears separately for English and Filipino. An error-recognition item must not silently satisfy sentence-structure coverage; a vocabulary question embedded in a passage is not automatically a reading-inference assessment. A tag is a proposed mapping until the complete item is reviewed.

Map eligible item versions to these nodes before assigning quotas. Count conceptual, applied, multi-step and unfamiliar-transfer tasks separately; one item can support several prerequisites but counts once toward 2,000. Reserve entire passage/fact/solution families across teaching and held-out evaluation, not merely different IDs. Reading-family holdouts need new texts; math transfer needs changed representations and unknowns, not just different numbers.
